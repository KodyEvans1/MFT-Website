# Google measurement review — November 25, 2026 proposal

## Decision and scope

**Status: BLOCKED pending classification of the exact event and recipient-visible data.** This document defines a conditional proposal for review; it does not approve collection, activate a route, establish legal compliance, or report a successful attribution test. Primary sources were checked on November 25, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles; November 26 UTC). The independent implementation passed the local verification recorded below. Production collection remains blocked; deployment, Google receipt and Ads credit are separate results.

The November 24 block was a deliberate exclusion, not an environment-flag defect. Blanket inferences that every public healthcare visit is PHI, that the customer-data policy prohibits all ordinary healthcare conversions, or that disabling Google Signals eliminates all linked-Ads measurement are overbroad and cannot justify the block. The checked-in November 24 assessment already qualified the first two points and acknowledged the partial vacatur of the HHS guidance. The actual-runtime probe does overturn a narrower implementation assumption: a configured generic GA4 title and a GA4-collector-only request model do not describe everything the linked Google runtime attempts to disclose. Reusing the conditional implementation in PR #26 does not approve production collection.

The proposal is limited to a generic new-client scheduling handoff from `https://www.mft.care/` or `https://www.mft.care/about/`, represented by the existing event name `simplepractice_exit`. Its meaning is an outbound navigation toward scheduling, not an appointment request submitted, appointment booked, treatment received, or financial outcome.

Google does not impose a blanket ban on every ordinary conversion measurement feature merely because the advertiser provides mental-health services. The specific unresolved restriction is Google's rule against Analytics data revealing sensitive information about a user, together with its prohibition on exposing protected health information (PHI). A scheduling-intent event associated with advertising/browser identifiers and the known practice can carry care-seeking meaning even without names or appointment details. The exact combination, rather than the event's spelling alone, requires classification. [G1][G2][G3]

Until that classification supports this exact flow, keep this Google event path blocked. A fresh advertising consent, successful request, neutral title, encoded route, or absence of a Google account warning is not a substitute for that determination.

### Exact-title checks and their limit

The revised proposal requires rejecting an unexpected title before the vendor loads, rechecking before application handoffs, and halting on a subsequent title mutation. A `MutationObserver` is asynchronous; it is not an outbound-request firewall and cannot by itself prove that a vendor will not read/send a changed title within the same JavaScript turn. Test immediate title mutation followed by a handoff, history change, focus loss, and unload, including replacement of the title element. If an excluded title escapes in a controlled test, report the implementation as failing that boundary. Do not claim that the standard runtime confines every request merely because the observer eventually halts it.

### Diagnosed post-disable requests and enforced stop policy

A later unchanged-vendor probe diagnosed a separate implementation defect: sampled `/td` diagnostics were initiated after `ga-disable-G-BTK39BBNRE` had been set during title-change/pagehide enforcement. Clearing the application queue, disabling GA collection and removing the loader did not stop every request from already executing vendor code. The earlier passing sample did not establish a complete stop boundary. This finding changes the implementation and required evidence; it does not approve the event or resolve its care-seeking association.

The corrective conditional runtime synchronously appends one new, fully populated enforced CSP `meta` directly to `head` when stopping a document that has started loading the vendor. The additional policy excludes Google from script, image, connection and frame sources, uses a restrictive default, and denies workers. It retains the approved first-party, Ops and SimplePractice sources. The existing GA disable flag, event-queue shutdown and website-state cleanup remain. No denied-consent ping is deliberately sent. Stopping itself does not reload the page or close the scheduling dialog.

HTML's insertion algorithm parses and enforces a populated CSP meta in `head`. CSP adds that policy to the document's policy list; enforced policies intersect, and later edits to the meta's content do not loosen them. The timing boundary is **requests initiated after stop-policy enforcement**. It is not retroactive cancellation, withdrawal of already disclosed data, or termination of running JavaScript. [S1][S2]

The initial conditional response already requires `worker-src 'none'` and excludes Google frame origins. This avoids relying on a later parent policy to stop an independent vendor context. CSP inheritance uses snapshots for locally created child documents; a later parent policy does not retroactively replace those snapshots. This does not claim control of arbitrary pre-existing frames or workers. [S2] Google's CSP guide documents resource permissions for its features, not a promise that a dynamic policy cancels every outstanding tag operation. [G19]

A later fresh advertising choice validates the current eligible context, saves a new permission deadline and explicitly reloads into a new document. Removing or editing the stop meta is not the recovery mechanism. Another tab's grant does not reactivate the stopped document. The notice must explain the reload before that choice; refusal and native scheduling remain available.

The asynchronous title-observer interval remains: a request initiated before the observer or earlier lifecycle check installs the stop policy is outside this protection. Capture-phase checks registered before vendor lifecycle listeners reduce that interval for the exercised paths; they are not a universal same-turn interception mechanism. The revised probe must cover direct image, fetch/beacon, script, frame and worker attempts, sampled diagnostics, title mutation, pagehide, withdrawal, expiry, explicit regrant/reload and preservation of Ops/native scheduling. **Only Chromium has been exercised so far; Firefox and WebKit have not been verified.** The corrective Chromium probe passed the scenarios and deterministic future-transport checks recorded below. This is version-specific evidence, not a guarantee about untested engines or future vendor changes. Production Google collection remains blocked.

## Product rules and supported attribution mechanics

**Personalized advertising:** Google's health policy includes mental-health counseling and restricts advertiser-curated audiences. Its subject is targeting; it is not itself a universal prohibition on standard conversion measurement. [G2]

**Enhanced conversions/customer-data products:** Google's customer-data policy identifies its product scope, including enhanced conversions and store-sales uploads, and restricts sensitive-category measurement in those features. Health/medical information and some counseling conversions are listed. Those features remain excluded; their rules must not be generalized to all ordinary click-ID measurement. [G3]

**Analytics collection:** Google separately prohibits Analytics data revealing sensitive information about a user. For HIPAA-regulated entities it forbids exposure of PHI, even if outside Google's contractual PII definition, and states that Analytics offers no business associate agreement. Consent cannot override these product restrictions. [G1]

**Technical attribution:** For permissible data, Google documents GA4 key-event conversion import into Ads with a correct account link, auto-tagging, and preservation of the genuine GCLID. Dropping click context and retaining only a campaign/source label does not establish click-attributed conversion credit. The link's personalized-advertising option must remain off; Google documents that it defaults on. This document authorizes no link, import, or conversion-setting change. [G7][G8]

**Consent:** Any later eligible implementation requires a new, separate advertising-measurement choice. Set `analytics_storage`, `ad_storage`, `ad_user_data`, and `ad_personalization` defaults to denied before configuration or events. An earlier analytics choice must not be upgraded silently. `ad_storage` controls advertising storage; `ad_user_data` controls advertising data use, including measurement; `ad_personalization` must remain denied for this proposal. Denied `ad_user_data` limits click-ID-keyed exports to Ads. Use blocking before consent for this path, not advanced-mode cookieless pings as a presumed no-disclosure substitute. Preserve withdrawal, earlier refusals, and the site's GPC/DNT behavior. [G6][G9]

**Current Ads/Signals controls:** Google documents a transition beginning **June 15, 2026** under which Ads Consent Mode, rather than the Analytics Google Signals setting, governs collection/use of Google Ads cookies and IDs. Signals settings then govern association with signed-in information for Analytics behavioral reporting. Consequently, Signals off does not establish that linked-Ads cookie or identifier requests are absent. Google's update leaves exact dates for additional personalization/IP-control changes unspecified; do not claim an unverified rollout is complete. Keep `ad_personalization` denied regardless, alongside the documented `allow_google_signals: false` and `allow_ad_personalization_signals: false` controls. The privacy guide's join-beacon statement does not justify ignoring contradictory observed traffic or assigning an undocumented meaning to a flag. [G18][G20]

Google says conversion tracking can continue under restricted data processing. That further distinguishes measurement from personalization, but does not permit prohibited information or establish compliance with Washington law. [G10]

The prior PR #26 account readback reports property-wide advertising personalization off, linked-account personalization off, Google Signals off, enhanced measurement off, and `simplepractice_exit` designated non-personalized with once-per-event counting. These settings reduce uses or automatic collection; they do not resolve this event's classification. They are prior reported account evidence, not a fresh inspection or changes performed by the author of this revision. Record current account verification separately.

## Legal classification: limited questions, not assumptions

**HIPAA:** HHS prominently records the June 2024 partial vacatur of its guidance concerning an IP address associated with a visit to an unauthenticated public health/provider page. A public visit plus IP must not automatically be called PHI on that basis. The remaining guidance distinguishes ordinary public browsing from disclosures involving identifiable care, scheduling, registration, or health input. It also states that ordinary cookie banners are not HIPAA authorizations. This review does not establish M.F.T.'s covered-entity status or conclusively classify the proposed pre-scheduling event. [L1]

**Washington:** RCW 19.373 includes linkable information identifying a consumer seeking healthcare, including inferred associations; cookie, device, and IP identifiers can be personal information. If applicable, collection and sharing have distinct consent rules, subject to statutory exceptions. The law also contains information-specific exemptions, including HIPAA PHI and qualifying chapter 70.02 information. Do not assume that removing direct identifiers, using a public route, or being a healthcare practice resolves applicability. A determination that data is exempt from this statute would not itself make it acceptable to Google. [L2][L3][L4]
